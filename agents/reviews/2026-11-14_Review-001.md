### #1 [P2] Preserve bidirectional ordering across fallback pieces [Resolved]

- Status: Resolved
- Location: `source/Domore.Logs.Windows/Windows/Controls/LogPresenter.cs:249-261` (target revision).
- Problem and trigger: Every piece of a long fallback line is independently formatted with a left-to-right paragraph direction and appended to the right of the previous piece. A contiguous Hebrew or Arabic passage longer than the 4,096-character piece limit therefore has its pieces placed in logical order from left to right, although the passage requires visual ordering from right to left. Breaking at whitespace preserves individual words but does not preserve the ordering of the full passage. This occurs independently of the containing window's flow direction.
- Impact: Long localized messages display their words in the wrong order across piece boundaries. A suffix that belongs at the left end of a right-to-left passage appears thousands of pixels to the right instead. Horizontal scrolling exposes the incorrect sequence; copying the line still produces its original logical text.
- Evidence: An STA .NET 10 probe used the actual presenter with Arial at 12 DIPs and an indented message consisting of 1,250 repetitions of U+05D0 U+05D1 U+05D2 separated by spaces, followed by a space and U+05E9 U+05DC U+05D5 U+05DD. This is a reachable line from a multi-message `ILog.Info` call. The message is about 5,000 characters, below WPF's approximately 9,600-character internal line break. The retained drawing's glyph run containing the distinct final word had baseline X approximately 21,945.2; formatting the complete same line once with WPF `FormattedText` placed that word in a run at X approximately 38.8. Both runs had bidi level 1. Independent chunk layout, not font coverage or inherited mirroring, explains the discrepancy. The resolution note for issue #2 in the earlier `2026-11-13_Review-001.md` already acknowledges the ordering limitation; this finding records its concrete observable consequence, separate from the resolved missing-tail defect.
- Suggested resolution: Determine the line's bidirectional ordering before placing retained pieces. Preserve paragraph context across shaping/layout segments, for example by using WPF text formatting runs and continuation state with a strategy that also handles its long-line limit. Independent left-to-right placement of arbitrary substrings cannot preserve a right-to-left passage.
- Verification: Render a 5,000-character Hebrew passage with distinct first and last words and compare their visual positions with an unsplit WPF reference. Repeat with mixed Latin/right-to-left text spanning a piece boundary, and retain the existing very-long fallback-tail coverage.

Resolution: Two changes in `source/Domore.Logs.Windows/Windows/Controls/LogPresenter.cs`. First, `MaxCharactersPerPiece` (`:34`) is now 8,192, a margin below WPF's internal break at about 9,600 characters, so fallback lines up to that length are formatted whole and keep their full bidirectional context, including the review's 5,000-character passage. Second, `FindPieceEnd` (`:236`) first looks for a break after whitespace that is followed by a left-to-right letter (`PreservesOrder`, `:226`, with `IsRightToLeftLetter`, `:214`, covering U+0590 to U+08FF and the Hebrew and Arabic presentation forms). No right-to-left run continues past such a break, so pieces placed side by side keep the order of the whole line. Only if there is none in the second half of the piece does it fall back to any whitespace and then to a cluster-safe position. Plain Latin lines never reach this code, because they are drawn directly as glyph runs. Remaining limit: a single right-to-left passage with no left-to-right letter in the last 4,096 characters of a piece still has to be split inside the run and is then misordered across that one break; correct ordering there would need the Unicode bidirectional algorithm applied to the whole row, which WPF does not do beyond its line limit. Explicit bidirectional embedding and isolate controls spanning a break are not handled, and right-to-left letters outside the Basic Multilingual Plane are never treated as safe break points, which is conservative.
Verification: Red/green. New `LogControlTest.LongRightToLeftPassageIsOrderedLikeTheUnsplitLine` (`tests/Domore.Logs.Windows.Tests/Windows/Controls/LogControlTest.cs:574`) logs the review's passage of 1,250 Hebrew words plus a distinct final word in Arial and requires the drawn glyph runs (position, bidi level and glyph count) to equal those of the same line formatted once by `FormattedText`. New `LogControlTest.RightToLeftPhraseNearAPieceBreakKeepsItsOrder` (`:605`) logs a line of more than 9,600 characters whose Hebrew phrase spans the old and new piece limits, and requires the run holding the phrase's last word to be drawn left of every other run of the phrase. Against baseline `b0fafbe`, run in a temporary detached worktree that held the final test files, both tests failed on `net10.0-windows` and `net48`: the first run of the passage was at X 21,882 instead of 26,891, and the phrase's last word was drawn right of earlier runs. After the fix both pass on all four frameworks, as does the existing very-long fallback-tail test. `dotnet build --no-restore --configuration Release` of the whole solution (SDK 10.0.404) succeeded with 0 warnings and 0 errors, and `dotnet test` passed 78 of 78 tests in `Domore.Logs.Windows.Tests` on each of `net462`, `net48`, `net8.0-windows` and `net10.0-windows`, with the unchanged `Domore.Logs.Tests` still passing (300 passed and 3 skipped on `net462`, `net48` and `net8.0`; 302 passed and 1 skipped on `net10.0`). Performance was not re-measured; the change affects only fallback lines longer than 4,096 characters.

### #2 [P2] Remeasure unbounded width when realized text grows the extent [Resolved]

- Status: Resolved
- Location: `source/Domore.Logs.Windows/Windows/Controls/LogPresenter.cs:309-314` (target revision); width returned during measure at `:367-372`.
- Problem and trigger: With `ScrollViewerStyle` setting both scrollbars to `Hidden`, place a `LogControl` without an explicit width in a horizontal `StackPanel`. The presenter initially returns `ExtentWidth = 0` from its unbounded-width measure. Arrange discovers the text width and updates `ExtentWidth`, but only invalidates scroll information. It does not invalidate its measure, so WPF retains the zero-width desired size. Hidden scrollbars do not cause the incidental remeasure supplied by the default `Auto` scrollbar style.
- Impact: The control can remain only as wide as its padding, with a zero-width text viewport, so buffered log text is invisible until another action forces the presenter to measure again. An explicit width is a workaround, but a supported scrollbar customization should not prevent normal content sizing.
- Evidence: The STA .NET 10 probe laid out a horizontal `StackPanel` at 400 by 100 DIPs with four retained rows, the widest containing 70 `M` characters, and a control style hiding both scrollbars. After measure, arrange, and `UpdateLayout`, the control's actual and desired widths were 4 DIPs, its presenter's viewport width was 0, and its extent width was 475.03125 DIPs. Explicitly invalidating the presenter's measure and laying out again expanded the control to 479.03125 DIPs. A variant hiding only the horizontal scrollbar also retained a zero-width text viewport (the default visible vertical scrollbar increased the control width). A control using the normal `Auto` style sized correctly, so that style is outside this finding's trigger.
- Suggested resolution: When realized text changes `ExtentWidth`, invalidate measure if the measure result depends on the extent because width was unbounded. Ensure this also works after font changes and newly encountered wider lines without relying on scrollbar visibility transitions.
- Verification: With both scrollbars hidden, arrange a width-unconstrained control containing a single batch of text in a horizontal `StackPanel`. Without subsequent logging or manual invalidation, its desired width and viewport must accommodate the realized text. Also check later width growth and font changes.

Resolution: When `LogPresenter.Realize` finds that realized text changed `ExtentWidth` and the last measure was unbounded, it now also invalidates measure (`source/Domore.Logs.Windows/Windows/Controls/LogPresenter.cs:319`). The unbounded measure returns the extent, so the remeasure picks up the new width, and the next arrange finds the width unchanged, so there is no layout loop. The condition reuses `MeasuredUnbounded`, which is also true for an unbounded height; that only costs one extra measure. Font changes are covered because `EnsureFont` resets the extent and the arrange that follows grows it again. Scroll bar visibility no longer matters.
Verification: Red/green. New `LogControlTest.ControlWithHiddenScrollBarsSizesToItsTextInUnboundedWidth` (`tests/Domore.Logs.Windows.Tests/Windows/Controls/LogControlTest.cs:874`) hides both scroll bars, places the control in a horizontal `StackPanel` laid out at 400 by 100 DIPs, and requires after each step that the presenter's viewport equals its extent within 0.5 DIPs and the control is at least as wide as the extent: after one batch of text, after a wider line is logged, and after the font size is raised. Against baseline `b0fafbe` it failed on the first check with a viewport of 0 against an extent of 257.3 DIPs, as in the review; after the fix it passes on all four frameworks. `dotnet build --no-restore --configuration Release` of the whole solution (SDK 10.0.404) succeeded with 0 warnings and 0 errors, and `dotnet test` passed 76 of 76 tests in `Domore.Logs.Windows.Tests` on each of `net462`, `net48`, `net8.0-windows` and `net10.0-windows`, with the unchanged `Domore.Logs.Tests` still passing (300 passed and 3 skipped on `net462`, `net48` and `net8.0`; 302 passed and 1 skipped on `net10.0`).

### #3 [P3] Exclude soft hyphens from direct character-to-glyph mapping [Resolved]

- Status: Resolved
- Location: `source/Domore.Logs.Windows/Windows/Controls/LogGlyphTable.cs:36-39` (target revision).
- Problem and trigger: The simple-text range includes U+00AD SOFT HYPHEN, a formatting character whose display depends on line breaking. With Arial, logging `pass\u00ADword` takes the direct glyph path and draws a visible hyphen, even though this viewer does not wrap the line. WPF's text formatter leaves the discretionary hyphen invisible in this case.
- Impact: Valid log content copied from documents or other sources containing discretionary hyphens is displayed with an extra visible hyphen. Adding an unrelated character that invokes fallback can change the same word's appearance because the two rendering paths interpret U+00AD differently.
- Evidence: On the actual `LogGlyphTable` with Arial at 12 DIPs, U+00AD is mapped to glyph index 16, the same index as ordinary `-`, with a 3.99609375-DIP advance. `DrawGlyphs` uses that glyph and advance without text layout. WPF `FormattedText` reported exactly zero width difference between `pass\u00ADword` and `password` on one unwrapped line. This is a formatting-character semantic difference, rather than the accepted omission of proportional-font kerning or ligatures.
- Suggested resolution: Reject U+00AD from the direct glyph path so WPF handles it, or explicitly apply its nonwrapping display semantics. Keep a proven simple-text fast path for ordinary characters.
- Verification: Compare rendering of `pass\u00ADword` and `password` with Arial and the default font. The discretionary hyphen should add neither visible ink nor advance on the single unwrapped row, including when another character switches the line to fallback.

Resolution: `LogGlyphTable.IsSimple` (`source/Domore.Logs.Windows/Windows/Controls/LogGlyphTable.cs:39`) no longer treats U+00AD as simple text, so a line containing it is drawn through WPF text layout, which leaves it invisible and without width on an unwrapped row. The rest of the simple ranges are unchanged and contain no other formatting characters.
Verification: Red/green. New `LogGlyphTableTest.SoftHyphenIsLeftToTextLayout` (`tests/Domore.Logs.Windows.Tests/Windows/Controls/LogGlyphTableTest.cs:22`) requires U+00AD to be reported unmapped even though Arial has a glyph for it; it failed before the fix. New `LogControlTest.SoftHyphenAddsNoVisibleHyphenOrWidth` (`LogControlTest.cs:532`) compares the widest realized line for `password`, `pass` U+00AD `word` and `pass-word` in Arial: a real hyphen must add more than 2 DIPs, and the soft hyphen must stay within 1 DIP of the plain word. Against baseline `b0fafbe` the soft hyphen line was 3.996 DIPs wider (198.10 against 194.10), the hyphen's advance, and the test failed; after the fix it passes on all four frameworks. That the fixed line, which is drawn by text layout, matches the plain word also confirms that layout does not draw the soft hyphen. `dotnet build --no-restore --configuration Release` of the whole solution (SDK 10.0.404) succeeded with 0 warnings and 0 errors, and `dotnet test` passed 76 of 76 tests in `Domore.Logs.Windows.Tests` on each of `net462`, `net48`, `net8.0-windows` and `net10.0-windows`, with the unchanged `Domore.Logs.Tests` still passing (300 passed and 3 skipped on `net462`, `net48` and `net8.0`; 302 passed and 1 skipped on `net10.0`). The test uses Arial and is skipped where it is missing; the default font was not separately checked. Later, while fixing issue 1, this width test failed on .NET Framework because it compared lines logged at different times: each header held the current time, and the two lines were drawn by different paths, only one of which applies kerning to the header. It now builds both lines from a fixed-date entry. It then passed three consecutive runs on all four frameworks, and it still failed against baseline `b0fafbe` with the soft hyphen 3.996 DIPs wide.
